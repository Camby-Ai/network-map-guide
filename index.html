Network Map · Build Guide

00 / Your network, on one map

Everyone you know.
One private map.

You already know the person who can open the door. You cannot see them, because your people are split across your phone, your inbox, your calendar and three social apps. This puts them on one map, shows who is tied to whom and why, and finds the warm path to the person you want to meet.

This is not a download. It is a build brief: the instructions I would hand an engineer, written so your own AI can build it from your own data, on your own computer. It is built the way mine is, and none of my data is in it. The finished map runs on your own computer.

01 / What you get

Seven views.
One page.

  • The globe. Every city where you know someone is a pillar. The more people, the taller it stands. Click one and you fly in.
  • City lists. Everyone in that city, sorted by who you are closest to.
  • The file on each person. Role, how you met, the last time you talked and on which channel: text, email, calendar or a social app.
  • Search. One box over names, titles, companies, industries, cities and your own tags.
  • The graph. Who is tied to whom. Every line carries its proof: a shared group chat, an email thread, a shared company.
  • Connectors. The people tied to the most of your people, with proven ties counted before guessed ones.
  • Warm paths. Type what you need, such as a role or an industry. You get the people who match and who you already know that can open the door.

03 / How to build it

Six steps.
Your AI does the typing.

01

Make one folder and open your AI in it

Use an AI that can read files and run code on your computer, such as Claude Code. Everything stays inside this folder.

02

Put your contacts export in it

One CSV. Add the other exports from section 02 when you have them. Do not wait for all of them.

03

Paste the build brief

It is in section 04 below. Copy all of it and paste it as your first message.

04

Answer its two questions

It will ask how you sort people today: your stages, such as inner circle, friends and new contacts, and any tags you use for people you trust. That is what “closest first” is built on. If you have neither, say so and it will sort by how recently you were in touch.

05

Check the counts after every step before you say go on

The brief makes your AI stop and report what matched, what did not and what it assumed. Read ten sample rows. If a name is matched to the wrong person, say so now. It is much harder to see later.

06

Open your map

The page loads its data from the folder, so the folder has to be served. Run this in the folder, then open the address in your browser.

Run in the folder
python3 -m http.server 8000 --bind 127.0.0.1
Then open
http://localhost:8000/map.html

The last part of that command keeps the page on your own computer. Other devices on your network cannot open it.

04 / The build brief

Copy this.
Paste it into your AI.

This is the whole specification: the rules, the folders, how people are matched across sources, how closeness and links are scored, and what each view does. It tells your AI to ask before it reads anything outside the folder.

Paste into your AI
BUILD BRIEF: PERSONAL NETWORK MAP

Build me a private, local tool that puts everyone I have been in contact with on one map, shows how they are tied to each other, and finds warm paths to people I want to meet. Work in this folder. Ask me before you read any file outside it.

HARD RULES
1. Local only. The build script reads local files and makes no network calls. No geocoding API, no enrichment API, no model calls on my contacts.
2. Read only. Never write to my contacts app, my messages, my mail or my calendar.
3. Never read message bodies or note text. Use metadata only: who, when, how many, the name of a group chat, the subject of a thread.
4. Phone numbers and email addresses are for matching only. They never appear in the output files.
5. Nothing is invented. A missing field shows as "Not on record". A location written as a guess ("likely", "unknown") stays unplaced.
6. Never create a person from a stranger in a thread. Only match to people I already have.
7. Keep a list of what the data cannot tell me (the gaps) and show it in the page.
8. In this chat, show me counts and at most ten sample rows. Never print my whole contact list here.

FOLDERS
data/contacts/   my contacts or CRM export, CSV, one row per person
data/edges/      who appeared together: imessage.jsonl, gmail.jsonl, calendar.jsonl
data/social/     Instagram followers and following, as a TSV
tools/           one small script per source
build.py         merges everything and writes out/
out/data.json    people, links, cities, meta
map.html         the whole front end, one file

STACK
- Python 3, standard library only (csv, json, sqlite3, zipfile, re).
- One HTML file. d3 version 7 and topojson from a CDN. No framework, no bundler, no build step.
- The globe is d3.geoOrthographic drawn on a 2D canvas. The graph is d3.forceSimulation on the same canvas. No WebGL.
- The page loads out/data.json with fetch, so the folder has to be served, on this computer only: python3 -m http.server 8000 --bind 127.0.0.1

DATA MODEL
person: id, name, title, company, industry, city, raw location, stage, met (yes or no), tags, groups, lanes, how we met, last touch "YYYY-MM-DD (channel)", last date per channel, sources, closeness score
link:   [person a, person b, type, confidence 0 to 3, evidence in one line]
city:   [key, label, lat, lon]
meta:   counts, lanes, gaps, when it was built

MATCHING PEOPLE ACROSS SOURCES
- The contacts export is the spine. Every other source is joined onto it.
- Match in this order: email, then phone (last 10 digits), then full name only when exactly one person has that name.
- Merge two records with the same full name only if the name has at least two words and seven letters and their companies do not conflict.
- Count what did not match and report it. Do not guess.

LAST TOUCH
- For each person keep the latest date per channel: text, email, calendar, call, and each social app my export has a date for.
- Last touch is the newest of those, with the channel named.

CLOSENESS (sort every list by this, closest first)
- Points for the relationship stage I have recorded for the person.
- Points for the trust tags I use.
- Recency: more points under 90 days, fewer under a year, a few under three years.
- Minus points for people I have not met yet.
Ask me for my stages and tags before you write this.

CITIES
- Use an offline table of cities: name, aliases, lat, lon. Start with the cities that appear in my data.
- Place a person only when their location text matches a city in the table.
- Let me correct a city in the page. Save corrections to a local overrides file, never back to my contacts app.

LINKS AND EVIDENCE
Keep one link per pair and type, at the highest confidence:
3  my contacts app says they are related
2  one is named in my notes about the other; both are tagged on the same note; they share small group chats, email threads or calendar events (8 people or fewer, or three or more shared)
1  the same, in bigger groups; same current company (skip any company with more than 25 of my contacts)
0  same past employer or school; same small group in my contacts app
Evidence is one short line, for example: 3 shared group chats, latest 2025: "<chat name>" (6 people)
"Evidenced" means 2 or 3. "Inferred" means 0 or 1. The page shows evidenced links by default.

SOURCES (one script each, all read only)
- Contacts or CRM: the CSV export.
- Messages on a Mac: ~/Library/Messages/chat.db, opened read only. Group chats with 2 to 25 members and at least 3 messages: chat name, last date, count, members. One to one chats: last date and count. Resolve handles through the Contacts database. No message text.
- Mail and calendar: for each thread or event, the date, the subject or title, and the participants.
- Instagram: the official "Download your information" file. Open only the followers and following files. Label each handle: follow each other, follows you, you follow.
- LinkedIn: the official data export. Open only the connections file: name, company, title, date connected.
- Anything a platform will not export, I will type into a spreadsheet myself. Do not scrape any site.

THE PAGE
1. Globe. Every city is a pillar that grows with the number of people there. Click a city to fly in and list its people, closest first.
2. Dossier. Click a person: role, stage, last touch, last date per channel, where they were found, and their links with the evidence.
3. Search. One box over name, title, company, industry, location and tags. Cities match too.
4. Graph. Everyone as a dot, links as lines, grouped by lane. Pick one person and light up their circle.
5. Connectors. Rank people by how many of my people they are tied to. Count evidenced links before inferred ones.
6. Warm paths. I type what I need: a role, an industry, a company. Targets are the people who match. A door is someone I have met who has a link to a target. Show me, the door, the target, and the evidence for each hop.
7. Data notes. The gaps list.

BUILD ORDER
1. Contacts export to people. Show me the counts and ten sample rows before going on.
2. Cities and the globe.
3. Messages, mail and calendar to links.
4. Dossier, search and graph.
5. Connectors and warm paths.
6. Social exports.
After each step tell me what matched, what did not, and what you assumed.

Prefer a file? Open the brief as plain text.

06 / Be honest about it

What it cannot tell you

The limits in my own build
LimitWhat happensWhat to do
Cities About one in five of my people has a city on record, so about one in five is on the globe Fix a city on the page when you notice it. The correction is saved locally
Roles About a third of my people have a title or company Search and warm paths only see what is written down. Fill in the people who matter
Instagram The export lists followers and following. It does not say who you have in common with someone Use it to see who follows you back, not as proof of a tie
Facebook It does not export who you have in common with someone Type the ones that matter into a sheet
Matching Two records join only on an email, a phone number or a name that exactly one person has Some real matches are missed on purpose. A wrong merge is worse
Search It matches the words you type against what is on record. It is not an AI guessing who you mean Type the word a person would have in their title, industry or tags

A map built from a thin address book is a thin map. The value comes from the notes you already keep on people, and it grows every time you add one.
